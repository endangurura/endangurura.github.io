All code conditions target 20 variants. GPT-OSS and Mistral coverage counts trials with an extractable sabotage probability; it does not measure completion of every audit requirement. Mistral has five trials per variant, so its valid-probability counts are not distinct-case counts. Qwen coverage counts variants with a retained valid probability after retries, not all executions. These are not directly comparable first-attempt success rates.

Benchmark-accepted fixes

Qwen achieved weighted top-1 fix credit of 33.3% with restricted code access and 11.1% with full experiments available. Accepted cases covered sandbagging detection, subliminal learning, and multi-turn jailbreaking. A model judge awards full credit to a proposed correction that addresses the sabotage and correctly explains its mechanism. Top-1 scores the first-ranked proposal, using 0, 0.5, or 1 for no, partial, or full credit. Missing proposals receive zero; scores are averaged over sabotaged cases with equal weight per source codebase.

These successes demonstrate useful local-model alignment-auditing behavior. They motivate testing whether targeted fine-tuning and stronger elicitation can make accepted fixes more consistent and economical; those interventions and cost ratios were not measured here.

Interpretation and data

Missing reports are not imputed as 50%. Conditional scores on small observed subsets do not establish a model ranking or an effect of experiment access.

The paper-only and initial code-access aggregates were recomputed from published entry tables. The final Mistral and Qwen results use the experiment summaries; their per-trial tables are not included in that release.
